THE READING ROOMSample article · 2 min

Making sense of
health information

Try plain reading, then compare this same article with its original.

When you read about health, you might come across terms such as hypertension, blood glucose, or chronic disease. Everyday words can make the text easier to follow without stopping at every unfamiliar phrase.

Medical language describes many different things: a symptom such as dyspnea, a condition such as anemia, or a test such as a biopsy. The distinction matters. A definition explains a word; it does not tell you whether you have a condition.

Understanding starts with being able to ask: “What does that mean?”

Keep the detail within reach

You may see osteoporosis in an article about bones, or cholesterol in information about a blood test. Plain language can offer a starting point, while the original term helps you find more information and discuss it with a health professional.

Words such as prognosis and asymptomatic may be useful to explain too. Some words remain marked for an explanation. Select a marked phrase to see its original, meaning, context and source.
